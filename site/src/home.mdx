## What it does

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="Run pipelines" icon="rocket">
    Typed YAML, every step a container. Independent steps run in parallel; unchanged steps come from a cache.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Design what should exist" icon="puzzle">
    Draw a blueprint on a canvas. Forgeline writes the OpenTofu, previews the plan, and builds exactly that plan once a
    different administrator approves.
  </Card>
  <Card title="An AI team" icon="star">
    Agents with instructions, skills and a short list of tools, on an org chart, with monthly budgets that stop them
    when used up.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Missions" icon="seti:pipeline">
    Give an agent a goal. It reads, proposes fixes, waits for your OK, checks its work and reports. Every finding names
    the lookup that showed it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Watch your cloud" icon="magnifier">
    A map of your AWS account: resources inside the stack that made them, lines only where AWS states the link, and
    what needs a look.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Enterprise controls" icon="approve-check">
    Single sign-on, approval rules (two approvals, named approvers), per-service switches for the AI, and an audit log
    you can export.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## How it stays safe

- **Nothing changes your cloud without a person.** Every change is a proposal a different administrator approves, and
  exactly what was previewed is what runs.
- **The AI can never do more than the person who asked.** Agents work with the asker's role, and you choose per AWS
  service whether the AI may see it, only look, or propose changes.
- **Claims must be backed.** A mission's findings must name a lookup the agent really ran; the rest are left out.
- **Everything is audited**, including refusals, and the log can be exported.

Why it works the way it does is written down in the [design notes](/forgeline/design/001-execution-engine/).
